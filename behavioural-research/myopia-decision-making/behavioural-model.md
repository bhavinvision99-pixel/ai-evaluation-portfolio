# Behavioural model

The project treated the decision as an interacting system rather than a linear funnel.

## Four interacting domains

### Child
Capability, motivation, treatment burden, confidence, lifestyle and willingness to sustain the behaviour.

### Parent
Beliefs about necessity, concerns about treatment, risk perception, emotional response, resources and trust.

### Clinician
Confidence, communication behaviour, perceived commercial conflict and ability to deliver a difficult recommendation consistently.

### Organisation
Access, affordability, culture, values, policies and the service environment surrounding the decision.

## Decision cycle

The infographic describes a cycle involving:

1. trigger
2. emotional response
3. coping strategies around emotion
4. appraisal / evaluation / action
5. coping strategies around beliefs
6. beliefs and perceptions

The core idea is that decisions emerge from interactions among emotion, belief, context and action rather than from information alone.

## Necessity-Concerns adaptation

Horne and Weinman's Necessity-Concerns model was originally developed around adherence. In this project it was adapted to:

- the pre-adoption stage
- proxy decision-making, where a parent decides on behalf of a child
- a multi-year preventative treatment with delayed and partly invisible benefit

The case study argues that necessity and concern can move independently. Increasing urgency or evidence may increase perceived necessity without resolving concerns about treatment burden, safety, price or clinician motives.
