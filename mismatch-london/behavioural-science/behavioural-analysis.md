# Behavioural Science Analysis

## The behavioural problem

A dataset may identify a home as under-occupied.

That does not mean moving is desirable, simple or even possible for the resident.

The administrative framing:

> “Under-occupied three-bedroom property.”

The resident framing:

> “My home.”

This gap is central to understanding voluntary housing mobility.

## Behavioural mechanisms

### Loss aversion

Moving can involve perceived losses:
- neighbours
- routines
- familiar transport
- proximity to family
- GP and local services
- garden or outdoor space
- ability for family or carers to stay

These losses may loom larger than the benefits of a technically suitable alternative.

### Status quo bias

Remaining in the current home requires no immediate action.

Moving requires:
- decision-making
- searching
- paperwork
- planning
- uncertainty

This gives the status quo an inherent behavioural advantage.

### Place attachment

Homes are embedded in:
- identity
- community
- memory
- social networks
- everyday routines

A property that looks suitable administratively may be less attractive socially or emotionally.

### Friction costs

Even residents who would consider moving can face practical barriers:
- finding an appropriate property
- navigating the process
- removals
- decorating
- utilities
- affordability
- accessibility
- timing
- uncertainty about the new neighbourhood

This creates an important distinction:

> **Under-occupation ≠ willingness to move.**

and:

> **Willingness to move ≠ ability to move.**

## Choice architecture

A poor policy question is:

> “How do we get people to downsize?”

A better behavioural question is:

> **“What would a voluntary housing alternative need to look like for someone to genuinely prefer it?”**

Potential design considerations include:
- nearby alternatives
- genuine choice
- suitable accessibility
- assistance with moving
- financial support
- ability to maintain community ties
- clear information
- control over timing
- ability for family or carers to stay

## Behavioural principle

The objective should not be to optimise stock by pressuring residents.

It should be to understand whether **better-designed voluntary choices** can create outcomes that benefit both residents and the wider housing system.

> **Don't optimise homes. Optimise choices.**
